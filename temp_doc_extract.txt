000 [Normal] Review of Related Literature
001 [Normal] This chapter presents a thematic review of related literature and studies that bear on the development of TOMO, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) smart personal assistant for Biglang-awa IT Solutions. The review is organized around five themes: (1) AI-driven workflow automation and the operational case for intelligent assistants; (2) Retrieval-Augmented Generation as an architecture for grounded, domain-specific AI; (3) conversational AI and knowledge assistants in Philippine organizations; (4) client acquisition, retention, and service delivery in knowledge-intensive firms; and (5) software quality evaluation under ISO/IEC 25010. Within each theme, local (Philippine) and foreign literature are read side by side, and each theme closes with its implication for the present study. Sources are limited to studies published from 2020 onward, consistent with the recency requirement for capstone research, except for a small number of foundational works on intelligent personal assistants
002 [Normal] (Han & Yang, 2018)
003 [Normal] (Hüsson, Holland, & Sánchez, 2020)
004 [Normal] .
005 [Normal] Theme 1: AI-Driven Workflow Automation and the Business Case for Intelligent Assistants
006 [Normal] Artificial intelligence has moved from an experimental technology to core operational infrastructure, and the international literature is nearly unanimous that its most immediate value for small firms lies in workflow automation. Al-Amin et al. (2024) describe how combining data integration, process mapping, predictive analytics, and robotic process automation consolidates fragmented operations, identifies bottlenecks, and frees employees from routine tasks, which increases efficiency, reduces errors, and strengthens competitiveness
007 [Normal] (TOMO, n.d.)
008 [Normal] . Their study of end-to-end workflow optimization for SMEs documents that SMEs typically depend on manual processes and fragmented data systems that limit their ability to scale and compete, making AI-assisted consolidation an operational necessity rather than a luxury
009 [Normal] (TOMO, n.d.)
010 [Normal] . Hüsson et al. (2021), examining small- and medium-sized service enterprises specifically, report average cost-savings potential of over 31% across relevant business processes when AI and intelligent personal assistants are applied, with personal-assistant features rated as especially useful by domain experts
011 [Normal] (TOMO, n.d.)
012 [Normal] . Complementing these efficiency arguments, a review of agentic workflow adoption between 2024 and 2025 found that organizations deploying agentic AI systems achieve 3 to 8 times higher automation rates in knowledge-work processes previously considered unautomatable
013 [Normal] (Maheen, 2025)
014 [Normal] .
015 [Normal] The foreign literature also clarifies what such assistants must look like to work in practice. Comparative experiments across three standard robotic process automation challenges show that rule-based RPA still outperforms LLM-driven agents in execution speed and reliability in repetitive, stable environments, while agentic approaches significantly reduce development time and adapt more flexibly to dynamic interfaces
016 [Normal] (Průcha, Matoušková, & Strnad, 2025)
017 [Normal] . An analysis of more than 6,000 publicly available low-code automation workflows similarly finds that LLMs are commonly embedded within broader automation structures involving control logic, external tools, communication services, and storage systems, but that explicit reliability mechanisms such as structured fallback paths and human approval gates remain uncommon, revealing a gap between deployment and engineering maturity
018 [Normal] (Tang, Zhou, & Chen, 2026)
019 [Normal] . This evidence cautions that a production assistant for a service firm needs deliberate workflow scaffolding, not only a language model.
020 [Normal] The Philippine literature mirrors this pattern while adding local constraints. A study of SMEs in Davao del Norte found that firms primarily adopt AI to enhance operational efficiency, reduce errors, and improve customer service, yet face barriers including high costs, a pronounced skills gap, data security concerns, and initial employee resistance, responding with small-scale pilots and incremental staff training
021 [Normal] (TOMO, n.d.)
022 [Normal] . Among 100 SMEs surveyed in Angeles City, 75% reported using predictive analytics, with AI significantly enhancing decision-making speed and accuracy and improving operational efficiency and profitability
023 [Normal] (TOMO, n.d.)

025 [Normal] (TOMO, n.d.)
026 [Normal] . An AI readiness study of 150 SME owners and managers in the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao found moderate technological and organizational readiness but low environmental readiness, with AI readiness significantly associated with business performance and organizational readiness showing the strongest influence

A UTAUT-based multi-group analysis of 387 Philippine MSME owner-managers further showed that firm size conditions adoption: performance expectancy exerted substantially stronger influence among medium enterprises than micro enterprises, while social influence showed the inverse pattern
029 [Normal] (Jr., 2026)
030 [Normal] . Taken together, these studies suggest that an AI assistant for a Philippine software consultancy must demonstrate clear performance gains on concrete workflows, since that is the adoption lever Philippine firms respond to most strongly.
031 [Normal] Theme 2: Retrieval-Augmented Generation as an Architecture for Grounded, Domain-Specific AI
032 [Normal] The second theme concerns the technical core of TOMO: RAG as an answer to the weaknesses of large language models used alone. LLMs alone suffer from hallucination, outdated parametric knowledge, and non-transparent reasoning; RAG addresses these weaknesses by grounding generated responses in external knowledge retrieved at inference time, enabling domain-specific accuracy and continuous knowledge updates without retraining the model
033 [Normal] (TOMO, n.d.)
034 [Normal] . The foundational survey of Gao et al. (2023) establishes this paradigm and its pipeline of retrieval, augmentation, and generation
035 [Normal] (TOMO, n.d.)
036 [Normal] . Empirical work confirms the magnitude of the improvement: an enhanced RAG framework combining vector search with semantic reranking reduced hallucination rates from 31% to 9% and raised accuracy from 68.4% to 91.2% in a technical knowledge corpus
037 [Normal] (TOMO, n.d.)
038 [Normal] .
039 [Normal] Foreign studies extend this finding in two directions relevant to TOMO. First, on enterprise grounding, an adaptive RAG framework for enterprise knowledge systems combining multi-source ingestion, semantic indexing, and metadata-aware retrieval improved Precision@10 from 0.58 to 0.81, reduced documentation retrieval latency from 45.6 seconds to 12.3 seconds, and shortened average bug-resolution time from 18.4 hours to 7.2 hours in a six-week pilot over a 1.2 million-document corpus
040 [Normal] (Baqar, 2026)
041 [Normal] . A hybrid sparse-dense evaluation using reciprocal rank fusion likewise found that the hybrid retriever achieves the highest accuracy, the lowest hallucination rate, and the lowest rejection rate compared with sparse-only and dense-only retrievers
042 [Normal] (Mala, Gezici, & Giannotti, 2025)
043 [Normal] . Second, on honest limits, systematic evaluations of alternative RAG topologies caution that the incorporation of external knowledge does not always align with the needs of the task: in one comparison, naive RAG and Graph RAG failed to outperform a fine-tuned model on benchmark question sets, indicating that retrieval design, not the label RAG, determines reliability
044 [Normal] (AboulEla et al., 2025)
045 [Normal] . A systematic evaluation of retriever and reranker pairings similarly found that the choice of retriever and similarity metric dominates system performance, while index selection exhibits a negligible impact across most metrics
046 [Normal] (Elkiran & Rasheed, 2026)
047 [Normal] . For TOMO, these works argue that architectural requirements analysis, the first objective of the present study, must be evidence-driven and empirically validated rather than assumed.

CHAPTER I 
INTRODUCTION 
 
1.1 Background of the Study 
A Learning Management System (LMS) has become a core infrastructure in higher education 
because it centralizes course content, assessments and communication between students and instructors 
(Turnbull et al., 2022). Research in different countries shows that while platforms such as Moodle, 
Google Classroom and other institutional LMS support flexible and data -driven teaching, they also 
present usability, design and policy challenges that affect how teachers and students actually experience 
online learning (Olaleye et al., 2023).  According to Olaleye et al. (2023) an evaluation of Moodle 
usability from the perspecti ve of teachers in a University of Applied Sciences in Finland found that 
interface design, navigation and analytics dashboards directly influence how instructors interact with 
the system and support student learning. The overview of LMS policies in higher education institutions 
reported that many universities have formal guidelines for LMS use but there are still gaps in how these 
policies are implemented and aligned with actual teaching and learning practices (Turnbull et al., 2022). 
These global insights suggest that adopting an LMS is not enough, the systems must be usable, policy-
aligned and responsive to the practical needs of educators and learners. 
In the Philippines, the rapid expansion of technology-enhanced and distance learning has led to 
increased use of LMS platforms in both basic and higher education.  According to Borabo (2024) the 
studies on the utilization of LMS in secondary schools from 2019 to 2023 reported that LMS platforms 
significantly shaped the implementation of distance learning, influencing access to materials, student 
engagement and continuity of instruction. At the higher education level , the effectiveness of LMS in 
universities such as the University of Science and Technology of Southern Philippines highlighted that 
LMS can support flexible learning but also identified issues related to system usability, support 
mechanisms and policy reco mmendations to improve institutional implementation (Roa et al., 2023). 
And in the recent work on the development of a Filipinized LMS further emphasized the importance of

1.2 Statement of the Problem 
The general problem of this study is the absence of a specialized Learning Management System 
for the Institute of Teacher Education (ITE) at Colegio de Montalban that centralizes learning activities, 
strengthens assessment integrity and supports effective communication between students and 
instructors. At present, academic tasks are managed across multiple separate platforms, which may lead 
to confusion, missed deadlines, difficulty in tracking academic progress. 
 
Specifically, it seeks to answer the following questions: 
1. How can a web -based learning management system be developed to centralize access to course 
materials, assignments, lesson plans, practicum documents, announcements and grades? 
2. How can a mobile learning application be developed to provide on -the-go access to learning 
resources, real-time notifications, submission updates, and academic records, ensuring continuous 
connectivity and accessibility for ITE students and instructors? 
3. How can a desktop -based administrative console be designed and implemented to serve as the 
central management hub, enabling secure user account administration, consolidated monitoring of 
academic records and practicum progress, system audit logging, databa se backup and recovery,

1.6 Scope and Limitations 
This study focuses on the development of a Learning Management System for the Institute of 
Teacher Education (ITE) at Colegio de Montalban. The system aims to centralize and organize 
academic activities to improve the management of learning materials, less on plans, practicum 
documents, assignments, announcements, notifications, and academic records. It will provide students, 
instructors, and authorized administrators with a unified platform for managing academic tasks. 
The system will include core modules for accessing course materials, submitting academic 
requirements and practicum portfolios, viewing grades, using AI-assisted guidance and chatbot support, 
receiving calendar -based reminders for deadlines, and managing s tudent and class data through 
import/export tools. It will also feature dedicated practicum supervision, video demonstration and 
lesson plan submission tools, and real-time notifications. The system includes basic user authentication 
and role -based access control to protect user information and ensure that users can access features 
according to their assigned roles. However, it does not include advanced security features such as 
biometric authentication or integration with external identity man agement services. Live video 
conferencing is included as an integrated feature for teaching demonstrations and supervisory meetings, 
but separate dedicated live streaming or broadcast capabilities are beyond the scope of this study. 
The system will be accessible through web-based platforms optimized for desktop and mobile 
devices. A dedicated desktop management console will serve as the administrative hub for user account 
management, data oversight, and system configuration. The mobile version will provide essential 
functions such as viewing grades, submitting requirements, and receiving updates, while full 
administrative and advanced features will be available through the web and desktop console. The study 
will follow the Agile Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC), covering  requirements gathering, 
design, development, testing, deployment, and evaluation phases. 
The respondents of this study will consist of selected ITE students and all ITE instructors. Based 
on records from the Office of the Registrar dated October 13, 2026, the total student population is 2,725.

LMS Platforms and Their Role in Higher Education 
Institutions choose among several platforms with distinct strengths. Moodle emerged as the 
most extensively studied open-source option, with a systematic review showing that it improves student 
performance, satisfaction, and engagement while supporting adaptive and collaborative learning across 
higher education institutions worldwide (Gamage, Ayres, & Behrend, 2022). Blackboard, by contrast, 
represents the commercial end of the spectrum, widely used in universities particularly in South Africa 
and the United States for online interaction, assessment, and course management (Badaru & Adu, 2022). 
Google Classroom occupies a lighter -weight niche, having proven effective and user -friendly 
communication, assignment management, and accessible learning materials, particularly during the 
COVID-19 pandemic (Sheelavant, 2020). 
When students evaluated platforms side by side, they preferred Moodle over Blackboard in 
terms of usability and system organization, indicating that the open -source platform provides a more 
user-friendly and structured learning experience (Alghafis, Alrash eed, & Abdulghany, 2020). At the 
premium end of the market, Paradiso ranked highest among 45 LMS platforms because of its strong 
interoperability, accessibility, communication tools, and security standards (Sanchez, Penarreta, & 
Soria Poma, 2024).  Spirin, Vakali uk, Ievdokymov, and Sydorenko (2022) established criteria for 
choosing a cloud -based LMS in higher education, focusing on usability, accessibility, and system 
effectiveness. 
Taken together, these studies suggest that platform choice is not a simple brand decision but a 
fit problem. The "best" LMS is the one whose usability, interoperability, and accessibility align with 
the needs of a specific institution. This directly suppor ts the premise of the present study, which 
develops a platform tailored to the specific workflows of one academic unit rather than adopting a 
generic system.

Usability, User Experience, and LMS Adoption 
Usability and user experience influence whether students adopt and continue using an LMS. 
Using the Extended Technology Acceptance Model, Fearnley and Amora (2020) found that these two 
perceptions strongly influence students' intention to adopt LMS platforms in higher education. Maslov, 
Nikou, and Hansen (2021) reached a converging conclusion through the lens of user experience, 
emphasizing that usability, accessibility, and system design strongly shape students' satisfaction and 
continued use. More recentl y, Saona, Alifah, and Zuhriyah (2026) demonstrated experimentally, 
through a quasi-experimental design, that better UX design significantly improves student motivation, 
collaboration, and learning outcomes, showing that design quality is not merely cosmetic but 
pedagogically consequential. 
In mobile contexts, performance expectancy, effort expectancy, and learning independence 
significantly influenced students' intentions to use mobile LMS platforms (Alfalah, 2022), and 
engagement and intention to use mobile LMS were similarly driven by moti vation, perceived 
usefulness, and ease of use (Imlawi  et al., 2023). Bai (2024) found that in hybrid learning, students' 
choice between mobile and non -mobile access is shaped by convenience, accessibility, and learning 
context, which implies that users do not commit to a single device but shift between them depending 
on circumstances. Kabassi et al. (2016) found that the effectiveness of an LMS used for blended learning 
in Greek higher education depended on usability, user satisfaction, and how well the system supported 
blended learning activities. 
 Küsel, Martin, and Markic (2020) compared university students in Germany and the United 
States and found that U.S. students showed higher readiness for digital learning, indicating that usability 
and design interact with users' prior digital preparedness. A system that is easy to use for one population 
may still overwhelm another.

experience are decisive for LMS success, and second, that because students move between desktop and

Online Assessment, Academic Integrity, and Learning Analytics 
LMS platforms support assessment, protect academic records, and help monitor student 
progress. Bulut et al. (2022) concluded that online formative assessments embedded in LMS platforms 
are strong predictors of students' final academic performance, while Ng uyen (2023) found that well -
designed LMS-based integrated online assessments increase learning motivation and improve academic 
outcomes among university students. Assessment within an LMS is therefore not just administrative 
convenience but a measurable contributor to learning. 
However, it creates an integrity problem that several studies address directly. Abdelsalam, 
Shokry, and Idrees (2023) proposed a blockchain -based framework integrated with Moodle to secure 
online examination data and prevent unauthorized changes to student records, treating tamper-resistance 
as a design requirement rather than an afterthought. Machine learning models can predict at -risk 
students at different stages of a course, enabling early intervention and improving success rates (Adnan 
et al., 2021), an d DeepLMS demonstrated that deep learning techniques can forecast the quality of 
student interactions within an LMS (Dias et al., 2020). 
Across the reviewed studies, an LMS should fulfill three important roles in assessment: support 
learning through formative feedback and motivation, maintain academic integrity and secure student 
records, and use learning analytics to identify students who may need additional support. The proposed 
system addresses these needs through anti-cheating mechanisms, secure assessments, and participation 
tracking. These features highlight the importance of combining effective assessment, data security, and 
student monitoring in an LMS.

LMS and Student Engagement, Performance, and Satisfaction in the Philippines 
Local studies consistently report positive outcomes from LMS use, mirroring foreign literature.

significantly boosts academic performance, participation, and satisfaction in Philippine higher

3.2 System / Project Overview  
The Learning Management System for the Institute of Teacher Education at Colegio de 
Montalban is designed to improve the efficiency, organization, and centralization of academic 
management processes, particularly for the Institute of Teacher Education. The  proposed system will 
provide a unified digital platform that can be accessed through web, mobile, and desktop applications, 
enabling users to perform academic tasks conveniently across different devices. 
The system will centralize the management of course materials, assignments, lesson plans, 
practicum portfolios, announcements, grades and feedback in one digital platform. It addresses the 
current difficulty of using multiple separate tools and manual processes where information is scattered, 
deadlines are missed, and tracking student progress and practicum requirements is difficult. Key 
features include secure user login, role -based access, calendar reminders, AI -assisted guidance, video 
demonstration submission, offline access and real-time notifications. 
The quality of the proposed system will be evaluated using the ISO/IEC 25010 criteria: 
functional suitability to confirm that all required ITE features are present and working , usability to 
ensure the system is easy to learn and navigate, performance efficiency to verify fast loading and stable 
operation and security to protect personal information, academic records and student teaching data.

and proportionality. Only information necessary for approved recruitment, needs analysis  and system 
evaluation will be requested. Access to enrollment lists, contact details or education records will require 
institutional authorization and the applicable lawful basis , research participation will not grant 
unrestricted access to student or faculty records of the Institute of Teacher Education (Republic Act No.

proceed only after the appropriate consent has been given. Declining to join or withdrawing from the
